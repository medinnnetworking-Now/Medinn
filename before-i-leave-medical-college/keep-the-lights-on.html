Article 002

Keep the lights on.

A student’s case for a campus that stays open to ideas.

Dr. Sidharth NarayananHouse Surgeon, GMC Kozhikode
November 2026 · 6 min read

Red a critiqueBlack an insightBlue the evidence

Watch the reelThis article goes with my Instagram reel. Watch it on Instagram.

When you get into a professional college, a medical college, even a government institution, you expect a platform. A place where you can pursue every interest you have: explore, create, debate, immerse.

That should be the basic promise. It isn’t a luxury.

We shouldn’t be limited by administrative issues. We shouldn’t be limited by infrastructure. This is basic.

What “basic” looks like elsewhere

Harvard, ranked #5 in the QS World University Rankings 2026[2], describes its Lamont Library in its own words as “open 24 hours a day.”[1] India’s best-ranked institution in the same rankings, IIT Delhi, sits at #123.

Closer to home, 24-hour reading space isn’t a foreign idea:

  • JNU: the central library’s reading hall has been open 24×7 since February 2014, after a long student demand. Students protested in 2018 when it was restricted, and in 2021 when reading rooms stayed closed after COVID.[3]
  • IISc Bangalore: the library lists itself as open 24 hours, Monday to Saturday.[4]
  • IIT Delhi: reading areas stay open till 3 AM, and round the clock during exams.[4]

What we have at Kozhikode Medical College

After all this time in our medical college, we have had to put up with a lot of limits. We don’t have open, free spaces. If you want to study well into the night, access runs only till a fixed hour, and past that you are not getting in.

The library and reading room close at 9 PM.

There was a movement among students to make the reading room 24 hours. It saw no result.

This isn’t the first time students here have pushed on after-hours rules. In 2022, students from our college, under the banner of Azadi – Break the Curfew, took the women’s hostel night curfew to the Kerala High Court (Fiona Joseph v. State of Kerala). Reports of the hearing say the court asked that restrictions on students be justified by compelling reasons and applied equally to men and women, and noted that because reading rooms and libraries here close early, students have little reason to be out late anyway. The petitions were later disposed of with the court observing that basic discipline has to be maintained in hostels.[5]

That case was about a hostel gate. It shows the same pattern: the hours are set by the institution, and the student has to argue for every extra hour.

It isn’t only about studying

Something I saw recently: our college band has its whole setup, the keyboard, everything aligned. Yet they don’t have a place where they can go and practise at 7 or 8 PM, after classes and everything else are over. A decent space, with decent lighting and a good atmosphere, still doesn’t exist.

And it goes beyond music. Whatever your interest is, research, entrepreneurship, discourse, someone talking like this, we need a place to explore a topic and sit and talk about it. All of this is very limited now.

The government has already said yes to the idea

Kerala’s Health and Family Welfare Department has approved Innovation and Entrepreneurship Development Centres (IEDCs) in all government medical colleges, and our college has launched its own.[6] As I understand the order, it calls for a dedicated space for collaboration and discourse.

The order exists. What students need to see is the space, open at the hours students can actually use it.

That is why this shouldn’t be the state of one of the best medical colleges in Kerala.

Why time and space matter: the reading period

Paul Graham, in How Universities Should Prepare Founders (2026)[7], writes:

“Reading period is the gap between the end of classes and the beginning of final exams.”

and:

“Microsoft and Meta have something in common that few people realize. They both got started during reading period at Harvard.”

His point is that this gap works because students are on campus and have nothing due the next day. Free time plus a place to use it is where projects begin. He also notes that giving students more free time is something universities may not like to do.

What I’m asking for

Before I leave medical college, I want to see some change happening. Either we open up more spaces, or we give our arts, our big events and our ideas the accommodation and the space they deserve.

Give us the space we deserve. Keep the lights on.

Say it anonymously

What would you use a space like this for? What does your college close that it should keep open? Tell it honestly. No name, no login.
