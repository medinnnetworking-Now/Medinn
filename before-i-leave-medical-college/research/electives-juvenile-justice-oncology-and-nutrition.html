What can one elective do for your research?

Two electives, and what they led to: a published paper, a published case report, and a report we chose not to publish.
Dr. Sidharth Narayanan iD
Dr. Sidharth Narayanan, 2020 MBBS batch

IN BRIEF

  • An elective can be your best chance to choose a topic and do real fieldwork. Ours were Community Medicine (juvenile justice) and Medical Oncology (nutrition).
  • Juvenile justice: courts, magistrate permissions, hearings, NGOs and case files. We presented it and made a report, but decided not to publish, because the data involved children.
  • Medical Oncology: a nutritional assessment study and a case report, both published in the Indian Journal of Medical and Paediatric Oncology.

One elective can seed several projects.

The electives are a rare chance to choose a topic and work in the field. We had two: Community Medicine and Medical Oncology. Below is what each led to, with a guide beside every step.

Just before final year: the electives

Just before my final-year postings and final-year classes started, we had electives that were made part of our programme.

The first elective: Community Medicine, sociology and juvenile justice

For the first block, I had Community Medicine. Our HOD and preceptor gave our team the rare opportunity to explore a topic of our choice.

I suggested that we work on sociology and criminal justice, and we eventually narrowed it down to juvenile justice.

  1. A rare opportunityAn elective may let you choose the topic. Use it.
  2. Start broad, then narrowWe began with sociology and criminal justice, and ended at juvenile justice.
  3. Ask your guide earlyOur HOD and preceptor backed the idea.

We went around the juvenile justice system and courts, trying to understand the system and its nuances, including the NGOs working in Kozhikode. We wanted to understand how psychiatric and community interventions were bringing down rates of recidivism and repeat offending.

I was very much involved in the process. We went from civil stations, obtained permissions from magistrates, attended actual hearings where children in conflict with the law were brought, and observed how juvenile cases were handled.

We talked to NGOs. We talked to social workers. We got access to case reports and case files of actual cases in the Kozhikode vicinity through the appropriate channels.

  1. Permissions firstWe obtained permissions from magistrates before attending hearings.
  2. Go to the fieldCourts, NGOs, social workers.
  3. Use the right channelsCase files only through the appropriate channels.

We understood that through NGO interventions and the Kaval programme, a lot of change had happened. This was presented in several forums, and a report was also made.

But due to departmental considerations and the sensitivity of data involving children, we decided not to push it or publish it.

  1. Not everything should be publishedData involving children is sensitive.
  2. Decide with your guideDepartmental considerations matter too.
  3. The report still countsIt was presented in forums, and a report was made.

The second elective: Medical Oncology

For the second elective, I was posted in Medical Oncology. To my surprise, my Medical Oncology professor, to whom I had previously discussed the topic of nutrition and cancer, posted the exact same topic for the Medical Oncology block.

The co-investigators started working on that project. We explored nutritional assessment tools to understand nutrition, how to investigate it and how to ask patients specific questions.

  1. Tell your professor what interests youThe topic I had discussed came back as the elective topic.
  2. Learn the toolsNutritional assessment tools, and how to ask patients the right questions.
  3. Join the teamCo-investigators share the work.

It gave me a revelation. Even before patients entered their long treatment periods, and even after they were diagnosed with a disease, there was no major intervention being done to help them in their dietary pursuits. I was left with that thought.

The project eventually took almost one and a half years to get published in the Indian Journal of Medical and Paediatric Oncology.

The case report from the same posting

During the same period, we also worked on a case report with a review of literature on a metastatic solitary fibrous tumour of the parapharyngeal region. Our guide, Dr. Sanudev, told us about an interesting case. It was mostly retrospective. We got consent from the patient at follow-up, collected the old records, the histopathology slides and the imaging, and had next-generation sequencing done. The first submission was rejected. We made the corrections and sent it again, and answered the reviewers one by one. It took almost a year to be published.

The step-by-step for the case report is in our guide, which is coming from CMC Analytica.

The people who made it possible

None of this was a solo effort. For the Medical Oncology elective, my collaborators were Dr. Varun, Dr. Punya and Dr. Shadi, with our guide, Dr. Sanudev. For the juvenile justice project, they were Dr. Varsha, Raniya, Shana and Sreelakshmi S.

  1. Share the workThe co-investigators started working on the project together.
  2. Cover each otherDifferent people bring different strengths.
  3. Credit everyoneSay who did what, and say it publicly.

How to use an elective

  1. Choose a topic you care aboutIf you can choose, choose.
  2. Tell your guide earlyInterests come back as opportunities. Ours did.
  3. Get permissions firstFieldwork needs them.
  4. Find your team earlyFieldwork, files, tools, data, drafts and reviewer replies are not a one-person job.
  5. Publish what you canProtect what you must.
