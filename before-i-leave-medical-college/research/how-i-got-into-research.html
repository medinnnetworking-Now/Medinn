Start from something that bothers you

Many students start with the portfolio: a way to go abroad, write the USMLE, or clear PLAB or AMC. That is a reason to start. It is not a reason to keep going when the data gets hard.

A question that bothers you is easier to carry. Three checks before you commit:

  1. Something you sawOn a ward, in a clinic, on campus.
  2. One sentenceIf you cannot say it as a question, it is not ready.
  3. Check it existsHas someone already asked it? That is your review of literature.

MY STORY

Why I did not see it as a portfolio

A lot of people get into undergraduate research for the sole purpose of building a portfolio so that they can go abroad, write the USMLE exam, or see it as a clearance aspect for their PLAB or AMC exams.

But I saw it from a different perspective.

It all started with how I got into MBBS in the first place.

The person who believed in me

I studied at PC Thomas Classes, and just before my drop year, I was influenced by an individual who believed in me quite a lot.

He told me all about whole-food, plant-based diets and veganism. He was a vegan himself. He talked to me about Dr. Michael Greger, Neal Barnard, and the greats who had been working on preventive medicine and the Physicians Committee for Responsible Medicine.

This was even before I understood the nuances of a medical college or had even seen a medical college for the first time.

He showed me Cowspiracy, Seaspiracy and all these movies that showed us what happens when you consume a lot of meat and other things that, in several instances, didn’t have solid scientific data behind them.

I was skeptical about a lot of this, but yet I listened to him because whole-food, plant-based diets and whole-food meals did have substance to them. And I kept that close to my heart.

The crossroads

I was at a crossroads in my life. I was very infuriated and washed away by the amount of time invested into NEET UG preparation during my 11th and 12th-grade schooling. I had enough of it.

After my first attempt, I had enough marks to get into veterinary medicine or BDS, and I had decided that I would take whatever I got.

But this individual and what he told me about whole-food diets, the power of preventive medicine and the influence of nutrition on life got me thinking.

I told my mom and dad that I wanted to give it another go. And that’s how I decided to drop and repeat for an MBBS seat.

I didn’t know then that these ideas would eventually become the foundation of my interest in research.

Then I joined college, and forgot

When I joined college, I was actually consumed by the need to fit in, to be validated, to be understood, to be loved, and to gain a place among my peers.

I discussed these ideas with some of them in the initial days, but I was ridiculed for a while. I guess I just pushed them all out and focused on the things that were happening and very much the hype at the moment.

Being isolated from college for a while due to COVID, then being back home, then back again, getting COVID twice, and trying to understand college dynamics, politics, arts and all the nuances of college life took up a lot of my attention.

I kind of let all of that aside.

I forgot about that guardian angel who actually took me to medical college. I forgot all the whys and hows of why I got there.

I put a lot of time into politics, arguing, understanding college dynamics and things like that.

But the overall atmosphere, when I look back, did not foster much academic thinking or even discourse.

There were a lot of pretty faces, a lot of pretty decisions that are a regret, but you could say that all these decisions led me to where I am right now.

Before you pick a topic

  1. Write downWhat bothered you on the ward this week.
  2. Pick oneThe one you cannot stop thinking about.
  3. SearchHas it been asked? Read before you plan.
  4. Then talkTake the question, not a topic, to a professor.
