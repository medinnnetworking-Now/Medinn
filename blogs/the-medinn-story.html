The Medinn story, so far

From a way of meeting people to the road to MedTech Malabar.

Dr. Sidharth Narayanan27 October 2026 · 3 min read · The first Medinn newsletter

Where it began

Medinn stands for Medical Innovation Networking. It began as a question I wanted answered: how does innovation actually happen in medicine? It was also my way of finding the people who could help me answer it.

I started in the place I know best, my alma mater, Government Medical College, Kozhikode, and the institutes around it. Within a short distance of the medical college sit NIT Calicut and IIM Kozhikode, along with many engineering and management institutions. The ingredients for medical innovation were all there. What was missing was a table where they met.

So I began with conversations: introductions, summits, panels and workshops. In 2024 I sat on the HealthTech panel at the IEDC Summit at NIT Calicut, on why healthcare needs engineers, and more recently I visited NIT Calicut's Centre for Innovation, Entrepreneurship and Incubation to start interdisciplinary projects with the medical college.

Then something bigger took shape

Slowly, the ground began to move. A makerspace opened right next to our college. Engineers come together there at all hours, even in the dead of the night, building things and working on problems. In September, medical students, doctors, engineers and makers spent a night there building for medical and surgical challenges, with more than 40 participants.

At the same time, an Innovation and Entrepreneurship Development Centre, the IEDC, was launched at Government Medical College, Kozhikode in May 2026. Students, faculty and physicians began to look at healthcare through the lens of innovation. I had pushed for it as an early advocate, and today I serve as one of its founding members and Lead.

“It was as I imagined it, but better.”

In October we ran India's first medical Repair Café. Condemned hospital devices from the medical college were opened up on one bench by medical students, engineers and makers. A patient monitor stuck on its boot screen was reflowed back to live vitals, a ventilator was taken apart and documented, and five devices were brought back to use.

The lessons went beyond the repairs. Many devices had no record of maintenance, donated equipment often never reached the biomedical team, and basic care such as changing a filter is something few healthcare workers are taught. As our report put it, we look after our phones and computers better than the devices patients depend on.

The road to MedTech Malabar

Watching all of this unfold, I cannot help but see where it leads: a future for our college and the cluster around it. I call it MedTech Malabar.

The vision is hospital ecosystems, engineering institutes and management institutes working as one, with a pipeline from ideation to prototyping, product creation and startups, right here in Malabar, for software and hardware alike. The baseline has been laid through Calicut Medical College and TinkerSpace, with follow-up collaborations at NIT Calicut. Incubation with a partner hospital is underway, and we are inviting people to be part of it.

Medinn is the strategist behind it.

What this newsletter is

This is the first issue of the Medinn newsletter. In the coming issues I will write about MedTech from the ground: devices, public healthcare, biomedical maintenance, why we need more biomedical engineers, and what it takes to build here.

If you are a hospital, an institute, an engineer, a mentor or a founder, I would love to hear from you. You can read more about the project at medinn-sandy.vercel.app/medtech-malabar.
