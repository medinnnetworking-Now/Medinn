## LinkedIn post (with the 12-slide PDF document)

What should a medical college really prepare us for?

The obvious answer is: to become doctors.

But as I get closer to the end of my time in medical college, I keep asking whether that should be the extent of what it enables.

We spend years learning medicine. But what about the students who want to do research, build medical technology, or solve the problems they see in hospitals every day? Should those interests stay extracurricular, disconnected from the institution where we learn medicine?

This is the first piece in a series I'm writing, Before I Leave Medical College: my own take on medical education, where it stands, where the gaps are, and what we could do better.

I'm not arguing that every student should become an entrepreneur, or that we need another compulsory subject. I'm arguing for an environment where those who want to explore research and innovation can, without feeling they are stepping outside medicine.

The full article is here: https://medinn.in/blogs/more-than-a-training-ground

I'd especially like to hear from medical students, doctors, educators and researchers: what should a medical college enable beyond clinical training?
