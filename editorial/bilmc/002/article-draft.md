## Written down, never used
*Why is the record of our training still kept on paper?*

MBBS is the foundation of every doctor's career. Right now, it feels undervalued. Part of what shows it is small and daily: how we keep the record of our own training.

**What we carry**

At Calicut Medical College, my training is recorded on paper. Logbooks for each phase. Record books for postings. Sheets to be signed, stamped and kept safe. I carried [N] logbooks and record books through MBBS, roughly [N] pages in all. Every one of them is filled by hand and signed by hand.

None of this is new, and I am not the first to feel it. But I have never heard anyone ask why.

**The phone in the pocket** (CRIT)

Every student I know carries a phone everywhere. We read on it, study on it, and learn at our own pace on edtech platforms built for exactly that. The same student then goes into a posting and records the day's learning on paper. The tools exist and they are in our pockets. The record is still paper.

**What paper cannot do** (CRIT)

A signed page tells you a signature happened. It does not tell the student what they have seen and what they have not. It cannot be searched. It cannot show a pattern across a year or across a batch. It cannot tell a teacher which skill most of a class has never practised. A logbook exists so that learning is recorded and guided. When it can only be signed, it is a record of compliance, not of learning.

**What the evidence says** (EVID)

I want to be careful here, because digital is not automatically better. Studies of electronic logbooks and portfolios are modest. A controlled comparison in Mashhad, Iran found no real difference in student satisfaction or final grades between paper and electronic portfolios. A study in Tehran that replaced a paper logbook with a web-based one found documented procedures rose from 92.7% to 98.5%. A study in Kurdistan found real-time monitoring and quicker feedback, but also extra workload and superficial completion. The lesson is not "put the form on a phone". It is that the record should help the learner, not just the signatory.

Digital logbooks and e-portfolios also already exist in India and are offered to medical colleges. I did not find a national rule that requires paper for MBBS. [Author: confirm against the current NMC notices before publishing.]

**The paper itself** (EVID)

Paper has a footprint. Published life-cycle studies put it at roughly 0.6 to 2 kg of CO2e per kg. By my own estimate that is about 7 to 8 grams per A4 sheet. [N] students times [N] pages per student, in one college, is [N] kg of CO2e. This is small next to a hospital's energy use, and I do not want to oversell it. But it is a choice repeated across every college, every batch, every year, and nobody has ever counted it.

**What we could do**

Strengthen the core first. A logbook is worth keeping. Start there:

1. Pilot one logbook digitally in one posting, with the students who are willing, and compare it honestly with the paper one.
2. Make the record useful to the learner: what I have seen, done and been taught, and what is still left.
3. Let teachers see the pattern, so that a gap in the batch can be fixed.
4. Keep the signature, but on a record that can be used.

None of this takes anything away from the training. It protects it.

**A question**

How many pages did your MBBS ask you to sign, and what could they have told you if someone had read them?
