# Before I Leave Medical College: the plan for the rest of the series, and the article pipeline

Compiled 2 November 2026 from (a) the series brief the owner gave, (b) the owner's Google Drive folder "Article Ideas & Drafts" and "Article brainstorm", and (c) titles and snippets of Notion pages. Read `TONE.md` before writing any of these.

## 1. The plan as it was set (what is actually on record)
- **Seven themes** the series will explore (from the owner's brief):
  1. Undergraduate medical education and the learning experience
  2. Clinical exposure, competency development and educational gaps
  3. Research culture and opportunities for medical students
  4. Medical entrepreneurship and technological innovation
  5. Collaboration between medicine, engineering and other fields
  6. Institutional support, mentorship and innovation infrastructure
  7. The future of medical colleges and healthcare education
- **Cadence:** about one article a week. Each article gets a 13-ish slide Instagram carousel, a LinkedIn PDF, one poster per "take" (a day each, each ending on a question), and conversations with readers. Every critique/insight in the article becomes a carousel slide or poster. Weekly cycle is in `001/captions.md`.
- **Order of argument for every article:** first strengthen the core (the value of MBBS and what we teach), then, for students who want it, expand what it can enable. Never "more than just".
- **Cross-feed from People of Healthcare:** PoH conversations should seed articles (e.g. a device founder says clinician-engineer collaboration is unstructured > an article on how medical colleges could host that collaboration).
- **No fixed article order was ever set.** The order below is a proposal.

## 5. Facts to verify before any of these is published
- CBME: the draft says "the NMC in 2019". The curriculum was introduced in 2019 by the then Medical Council of India; the NMC replaced it in 2020. Check the wording and cite the regulation.
- Logbook costs (Rs 100 to 300 per department per semester) are a personal observation. Present them as such, not as a statistic.
- The "US patient proxy forms" claim in the patient-education paragraph: needs a source.
- KAVAL recidivism "13% to 5%": quoted in the electives report; cite the primary source.
- Never name individuals, faculty or patients.
