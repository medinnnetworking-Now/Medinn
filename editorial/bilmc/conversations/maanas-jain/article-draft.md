## Meet him
Dr. Maanas Jain is Founder and CTO of Quintubate Pvt. Ltd. His Instagram profile lists MBBS at AIIMS Jodhpur and Internal Medicine at Rutgers University, USA. Before starting residency, he set out to build Quintubate. [Confirm timeline.]

## The gap (red)
A standard laryngoscope costs about ₹1,000 to 2,000. His own video shows a video-laryngoscope at ₹2,00,000 to ₹3,00,000. [Source: his video.]

## What he built (blue)
UVEAL attaches to existing laryngoscopes and turns them into video-laryngoscopes that send the view to a smartphone. Dr. Jain says: "In a span of 6 months since commercialization, UVEAL is being used by 250+ doctors across India and 12 other countries." The company states the device is CDSCO certified and starts under ₹10,000; his video shows a real intubation at Civil Hospital, Gandhinagar. [Verify CDSCO before repeating as fact.]

## In his words
**What are you building or leading?** "Quintubate is an initiative focussed on democratizing access to high-tech, expensive medical devices by manufacturing and developing existing, as well as novel, crucially important medical devices at a sustainable cost for Indian healthcare."
**What is your biggest project, and what highlights do you want the world to know?** "Our flagship medical device, UVEAL, attaches to existing laryngoscopes and transforms them into cost-effective videolaryngoscopes. UVEAL brings down the cost of videolaryngoscopy from the lakhs to the thousands..."
**What are the bottlenecks you face?** (critique, red) "The medical device ecosystem is largely limited to a select few companies, all based internationally, and hence healthcare in India is not supportive of made-in-India devices. Clinicians are not aware, and are skeptical of Indian medical device startups."
**What bridges need to be built?** "There needs to be an ecosystem for hospitals to learn about new licensed products developed by medical device startups as well as an incentive to adopt such indigenously designed devices. Regulators, and medical colleges need to be on the lookout and supportive to push for the adoption of such devices to promote a culture of development."
**How can we increase adoption and discovery?** "Fostering dialogue between medical device startups and existing hospitals, and having the opportunity to present the work to them would greatly enhance the chances of adoption."
**How do you see the future of healthcare?** (red) "The trend of medical devices is towards consolidation of all high-tech products in a select few companies, none of which are based in India. If Indian [doctors and hospitals] do not push for development and adoption of indigenous device adoption, medical device innovation in India will inevitably stagnate."
**What needs to be done?** "There is a big opportunity. Since very few medical devices are actually made in India, there is a huge scope for founders to pitch in and innovate to fill that large gap."
