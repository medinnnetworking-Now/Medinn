# Attendance (working title): research brief for the next article

Compiled 3 November 2026. Verified items are marked; anything I could not confirm from a primary document is marked CHECK. Read `../TONE.md` first. Nothing here is written as the owner's text.

## The author's seed (verbatim ideas, 3 November)
- Leave has to be justified, and taking it costs the stipend.
- If someone can afford to forgo the stipend on many days, they can skip postings "simply because they didn't want to go". Absence is shaped by money, not by what is learned.
- Postings can be monotonous and repetitive "all around the world". Hours of sitting (2 to 3 hours straight) are counted as learning.
- The author has not attended postings on multiple days. (See "Before publishing" below.)

## The thesis I would suggest (fits the premise)
**Attendance counts presence, not learning, and it is enforced through pay, so it measures who can afford to be absent.** MBBS and its postings are where doctors are made; the aim is to make every day in them worth attending, not to punish absence.

## What the evidence says
Mixed, mostly observational. Attendance goes with better results; forcing attendance does not clearly produce them.
- [Systematic review, 2025: changes in medical student attendance at in-person sessions (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12090850/): of nine studies on performance, five found a significant positive correlation; the review notes compulsory attendance may not produce the same correlation.
- [Compulsory lecture attendance: a poison or antidote? (The Asia Pacific Scholar)](https://medicine.nus.edu.sg/taps/issues/compulsory-lecture-attendance-a-poison-or-antidote/) CHECK the details before quoting.
- Review by Doggrell (2020) across 27 studies: a positive association between lecture attendance and outcomes in 75% of courses (82% in dental and medical). Observational. CHECK the citation before quoting.
- [Teaching methods that promote medical student attendance: a scoping review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40670-025-02535-0): mixed results.
- A single-school study reported that after attendance became compulsory in two first-year courses, attendance rose but no clear effect on attainment was seen. CHECK the source before quoting.

## How others do it (partial; each point has limits)
- **England:** resident doctors on the 2016 contract start with 27 days of annual leave, rising to 32 after five years' NHS service, and can take annual leave for life events with 6 weeks' notice. They are employees. [BMA: resident doctors' leave (2016 terms)](https://bma.org.uk/pay-and-contracts/leave/annual-leave-entitlement/resident-doctors-leave-2016-terms-and-conditions). Foundation-year specifics and sick pay: CHECK the 2016 TCS.
- **United States:** accreditation rules require paid medical, parental and caregiver leave from day one, at 100% pay for the first six weeks. Internal medicine board eligibility allows up to 35 days away per academic year for vacation, illness and family leave. [ACGME: resident leave policies](https://www.acgme.org/newsroom/blog/2022/acgme-answers-resident-leave-policies); [Mondaq summary](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/healthcare/1162400/acgme-mandates-paid-residentfellow-leave-starting-day-one). The 35-day figure came via a summary: CHECK ABIM.
- **Germany:** lectures generally carry no mandatory attendance; practicals can; German courts have limited blanket attendance rules. One university medicine curriculum dropped lecture attendance in 2016 (a single student-newspaper report). [Bavarian parliament document (German)](https://www.bayern.landtag.de/www/ElanTextAblage_WP17/Drucksachen/Schriftliche%20Anfragen/17_0017458.pdf); [Ruprecht student newspaper](https://ruprecht.de/2016/12/13/dreimal-fehlen-nichts-passiert). Medicine-specific current status: CHECK. Netherlands: not found.
- **Australia (student voice, not policy):** the Australian Medical Students' Association recommends removing 100% mandatory attendance and warns of a culture of presenteeism. [AMSA, Medical School Learning Conditions 2020](https://amsa.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Medical-School-Learning-Conditions-2020.pdf)
- **UK schools** log attendance as part of professionalism (single-school policies): e.g. [Buckingham absence and leave policy](https://medvle.buckingham.ac.uk/pluginfile.php/48/course/section/49/UBMS%20Absence%20and%20Leave%20Policy%20v6%20.pdf).
- **Switzerland (Zurich):** media reports of a medicine programme counting in-lecture questions rather than physical presence. CHECK.

## Proposed structure (strengthen the core, then expand)
1. **The value first:** postings are where a doctor is made. Presence matters.
2. **The rule as it stands:** 75/80 in the MBBS; the CRMI leave cap and extension; what the author's own college does (author to supply the order).
3. **What a "day" has become:** a number to protect, not something learned. Justifying leave. The monotony of repeated postings.
4. **Money as the gate (the critique):** when absence costs the stipend, the ones who can afford it skip, and the ones who cannot attend even when they should rest. Presence is decided by finances, not by learning.
5. **What the evidence says:** attendance and results go together; forcing it does not clearly help.
6. **How others do it:** paid leave as an entitlement (England, US); attendance tied to practicals not lectures (Germany); student calls against presenteeism (Australia).
7. **What we could do:** count what was seen, done and taught (a competency-linked log of exposures) alongside presence; a protected leave pool with sick leave that does not cost the stipend; make postings worth attending (objectives, supervision, feedback, rotation variety); consider flexible rosters; let students flag a day that taught nothing.
8. **Close:** attendance is a proxy. The goal is to make every day in the posting worth the student's day.

## Draft takes for the posters (proposals; to be matched to the author's final text)
1. (critique) Attendance counts the day. It does not count what we learned that day.
2. (critique) When leave costs your stipend, whether you can afford to be absent decides your attendance.
3. (critique) A posting that is worth a student's day should not need a penalty to fill it.
4. (insight) Measure a day on the ward by what we saw, did and were taught.
5. (insight) Leave should be a protected pool, not something we have to justify.
6. (evidence) Attendance and results go together. Forcing attendance does not clearly improve either.
7. (evidence) In England, a resident doctor starts with 27 days of annual leave. In the US, paid medical leave starts on day one.
8. (question) What would you count instead of attendance?
