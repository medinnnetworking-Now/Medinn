# Paper (working title): research brief

Compiled 6 Nov 2026. Read `../TONE.md` first. Nothing here is the owner's text. CHECK = not verified from a primary source.

## Author's account (first-hand, 6 Nov)
- Medical learning in Kerala is on paper: logbooks, documentation, everything. Nothing digital in the author's experience.
- Phones are carried by everyone; edtech platforms are used for self-paced learning, yet the paper load persists.
- Author believes engineering institutes have gone digital (UNVERIFIED, see below).
- Article 3 to follow: digital infrastructure, innovation, "why we are still stuck in the 80s".
- Publish as the author's account ("in my college and those I know"), not as a statewide claim.

## Suggested thesis
Medical learning is recorded on paper nobody can search, analyse or learn from. The record exists to be signed, not used. Carbon is the visible cost; the larger cost is the data we never get.

## Evidence
- Paper CBME logbooks are sold per phase and subject: [Jaypee](https://jaypeebrothers.com/products/9789354654169), [IAPSM](https://www.iapsm.org/pdf/IAPSM-Logbook-FINAL.pdf).
- Digital options exist and are marketed to Indian colleges ([eportfolios.in](https://eportfolios.in)); vendor claim that PGMER-2023 requires PG e-logbooks: CHECK the regulation. No NMC text found mandating paper or digital for MBBS: CHECK NMC notices.
- E-logbook studies are modest: [Mashhad](https://brieflands.com/articles/jme-103399) no difference in satisfaction/grades; [Tehran](https://rdme.tbzmed.ac.ir/Article/rdme-33336) procedure documentation 92.7% paper vs 98.5% web; [Kurdistan](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.943971/epub) real-time monitoring but superficial completion. Digital alone is not the fix.
- Carbon: paper life-cycle about 0.6 to 2 kg CO2e/kg ([Yale](https://environment.yale.edu/bibcite/reference/2822), [Victorian EPA](https://epa.vic.gov.au/-/media/epa/files/publications/1374-1.pdf)). Own estimate 7 to 8 g CO2e per A4 sheet (arithmetic, not published). Small next to hospital energy use: present as order of magnitude only.
- Engineering "paperless" claim: only ERP/admin projects found (e.g. [IIT Roorkee SAP](https://insidesap.com/indian-institute-of-technology-roorkee-kicks-off-sap-erp-project/)). CHECK before stating.

## Proposed structure
1. Why logbooks matter (core of MBBS training).
2. What is carried: the paper load, in the author's numbers.
3. Phones and edtech exist; the record stays on paper (critique, red).
4. Cost one: carbon (small, shown honestly).
5. Cost two: unsearchable, unauditable, unlearnable data.
6. What others do (e-portfolios, with the honest evidence).
7. What we could do: start with one logbook; pilot; student-led.
8. Close and bridge to Article 3 on digital infrastructure.

## Draft takes (each ends in a question)
1. (critique) We carry phones everywhere. We still record our training on paper. Why?
2. (critique) A logbook that cannot be searched cannot teach us anything. What is it for?
3. (evidence) Where logbooks went web-based, one Tehran study found documentation rose from 92.7% to 98.5%.
4. (insight) The record should help the learner, not just the signatory. What would you want it to show you?
5. (question) How many pages of paper did your MBBS ask you to sign?
