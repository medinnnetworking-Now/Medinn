One patient. Or many.

Case reportThe detailed story of one patient, usually a rare or unusual case.
2
Cross-sectional studyA snapshot of a group at one point in time. How common is it?

DESIGN 1 · CASE REPORT

Start with a case on the ward.

We see cases every day. There are always rare cases in wards. These cases have unique aspects that need to be covered, presented in forums and published.
I believe undergraduates should do this from the very beginning.

CASE REPORT · HOW

How a case report comes together.

1
Pick the caseSomething rare, or something that taught you a lesson.
2
ConsentTake the patient’s written consent before you write.
3
Follow a guidelineThe CARE guidelines give you the checklist.
4
Review the literatureWhat has been reported before, and what is new here?

WHAT WORKED · CASE REPORT

A metastatic solitary fibrous tumour, published.

We worked on a case report on a metastatic solid tumour with a review of literature. It took about one and a half years to get published.
Indian J Med Paediatr Oncol, Apr 2025. I was sixth author.

DESIGN 2 · CROSS-SECTIONAL

A cross-sectional study is a snapshot.

You look at a population at one point in time. It tells you how common something is. It cannot tell you what caused what.

CROSS-SECTIONAL · HOW

Draft the protocol first.

1
Question and objectivesWhat exactly are you asking?
2
Design, setting, populationWho, where, and how many?
3
Tools and variablesWhat will you measure, and how?
4
Analysis planWhich tables will the paper need?
5
Approvals and consentResearch and ethics committee, before the first participant.

WHAT WORKED · CROSS-SECTIONAL

A narrow question on one patient group.

The nutrition question I carried from second year became a nutritional assessment of cancer patients. Dr. Asma Rahim and Dr. Ashik Refeek told me to narrow it down. It took almost one and a half years to publish.
Indian J Med Paediatr Oncol, Mar 2026. Second author.

WHAT DID NOT WORK

We collected first. We understood later.

The campus NCD survey used WHO STEPS 1 and 2. We had approvals. But it took another one and a half years to get into the data.
Too much data. Unsegmented, without the tools or the team to structure it or publish it.
