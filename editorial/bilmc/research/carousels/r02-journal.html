DESIGN 1 · CASE REPORT

Start with a case on the ward.

We see cases every day, and there are always rare cases in wards. These cases have unique aspects that need to be covered, presented in forums and published.
I believe undergraduates should do this from the very beginning.

CASE REPORT · HOW

How a case report comes together.

1
Pick the caseRare, or one that changed how you think.
2
Informed consentWritten consent from the patient, before you write.
3
Review the literatureWhat has been reported, and what is new here?
4
Follow CAREPatient information, clinical findings, timeline, diagnosis, treatment, follow-up, discussion.

DESIGN 2 · CROSS-SECTIONAL

A cross-sectional study is a snapshot.

You study a population at one point in time. It gives you prevalence. It cannot tell you what caused what.

CROSS-SECTIONAL · THE PROTOCOL

Write the protocol first.

1
Question and objectivesWhat exactly are you asking?
2
Population and sampleWho, where, how many, and how will you select them?
3
Tools and variablesWhat will you measure? Use a validated tool.
4
Analysis planWhich tables will the paper need? Draw them first.
5
Approvals and consentResearch and ethics committees, before the first participant.

HOW THE NCD STUDY STARTED

A health camp. Why waste the data?

The then College Union was running a health camp. A lot of tests would be done.
Why not assess the risk factors of all the students on campus? We drafted a protocol on the WHO STEPS 1 and 2 programme, with Institutional Research Committee and Ethics Committee approval.

WHAT DID NOT WORK

We collected first. We understood later.

It took another one and a half years to learn to process the data with SPSS and Excel formulas, set by set.
Too much data. Unsegmented, without the tools or the team to structure it. We spread ourselves too thin.
GUIDE: report it with the STROBE checklist, and plan the analysis before you collect.

WHAT WORKED · CROSS-SECTIONAL

A narrow question on one patient group.
