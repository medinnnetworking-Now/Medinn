WHAT AN ELECTIVE IS

The electives are a rare chance.

Just before final-year postings, we had electives. Our HOD and preceptor gave us the rare opportunity to explore a topic of our choice.
GUIDE: if your elective lets you choose, choose something you care about.

ELECTIVE 1 · COMMUNITY MEDICINE

From a broad interest to one topic.

I suggested sociology and criminal justice. We narrowed it down to juvenile justice.
GUIDE: start broad, then narrow to a topic you can finish in one block.

ELECTIVE 1 · THE FIELD

We went to the courts.

We obtained permissions from magistrates, attended actual hearings where children in conflict with the law were brought, and observed how juvenile cases were handled.
GUIDE: permissions come before fieldwork.

ELECTIVE 1 · THE PEOPLE

NGOs, social workers, case files.

We talked to NGOs and social workers in Kozhikode, and got access to case files of actual cases through the appropriate channels.
We wanted to understand how psychiatric and community interventions were bringing down repeat offending.

ELECTIVE 1 · THE DECISION

And we chose not to publish.

Because of departmental considerations and the sensitivity of data involving children, we decided not to push it or publish it.

ELECTIVE 2 · MEDICAL ONCOLOGY

The topic I discussed came back.

I had once discussed nutrition and cancer with my Medical Oncology professor. To my surprise, he posted the exact same topic for the Medical Oncology block.
GUIDE: tell your professors what interests you. They remember.

ELECTIVE 2 · THE METHOD

Learn the tools, and how to ask.

We explored nutritional assessment tools to understand nutrition, how to investigate it, and how to ask patients specific questions.
GUIDE: use an established tool. Learn how to ask patients, not just what to ask.
