Most people do undergraduate research for the portfolio.
I did it because I loved reading, building on it, and building a narrative.

Before I leave med school, I wrote down everything I wish I had known: An Undergrad's Guide to UG Research.

What's inside, from my time at Calicut Medical College:
• A cross-sectional study on NCD risk factors that earned the KUHS UG Research Award, and never became a paper
• Electives that led to a published nutritional assessment study and a case report
• A juvenile justice project we chose not to publish, because the data involved children
• My first published credit: a peer review of an online preprint in JMIRx Med
• A 25-year retrospective analysis of obesity-driven cardiac mortality that got into the Journal of the American Heart Association (I'm the fourth author)

Each part is a story and a step-by-step guide: finding a question, the literature review, the protocol, the Institutional Research Committee and the Institutional Ethics Committee, consent, reporting guidelines (CARE, STROBE), rejection and resubmission.

The biggest lesson: orient your guide, plan the analysis before the data, and find your collaborators. None of this was a solo effort. Thank you to Dr. Jerin James, Dr. Sanudev, Dr. Ashik, Dr. Asma Rahim, Dr. Varun, Dr. Punya, Dr. Shadi, Dr. Varsha, Raniya, Shana and Sreelakshmi S.
