People of Healthcare
A Medinn initiative · Kerala

Meeting the people changing healthcare in Kerala.

As 2026 ends, healthcare is expanding every day: the rise of AI, a new focus on medical devices, and medicine and engineering working together like never before. I want to understand who is building this change in Kerala, and what is still missing.

Who I am

Dr. Sidharth Narayanan, a house surgeon (MBBS, Government Medical College, Kozhikode) and the founder of Medinn, which stands for Medical Innovation Networking. I work where medicine meets MedTech and medical education:

  • Founding member and lead of the Innovation and Entrepreneurship Development Centre (IEDC) at GMC Kozhikode, launched on 16 May 2026.
  • Organiser of India’s first medical Repair Café (18 October 2026), where condemned hospital devices were opened up by medical students, engineers and makers, and of the Medical & Surgical Intervention Hackathon (September 2026, TinkerSpace Calicut).
  • Strategist behind MedTech Malabar, bringing hospitals, engineering institutes and management institutes together.
  • COO of Cytokine Lectures, an edtech for medical students. Panelist on the HealthTech track at the IEDC Summit, NIT Calicut (2024).
  • Author of the series Before I Leave Medical College on bringing value back to undergraduate medical education.

Why I’m doing this

From inside a medical college I see clinical problems up close, and a great deal of capability around us that I can’t yet see clearly: who builds, who manufactures, what talent exists, and where good ideas stall on the way from a clinical problem to a product. I’m curious about those gaps. I want to understand them properly, and help build a stronger healthcare system in Kerala, from both a business and an innovation point of view.

The challenge: before 2026 ends, 50 conversations with the hospital founders, CEOs, MedTech and device makers, key opinion leaders, researchers and investors who are shaping healthcare in Kerala.

What I’d love to understand from you

PeopleWhat you’ve built, how it began, and who else I should meet.
ProductionWhat Kerala can develop and make locally, and where we depend on others.
TalentThe skills that exist, the ones that are missing, and how to build them.
InnovationWhat the road from an idea to a product really involves.
PipelinesThe funding, mentorship, partnerships and approvals it takes to execute.
Doctors as buildersHow clinicians and medical students can take part in a meaningful way.

What happens with what you share

  1. We talk for 30 to 45 minutes: by phone, video call or in person, whichever suits you.
  2. I write your story as a profile and send it to you first. Nothing is published without your approval.
  3. It is published and featured on Medinn’s People of Healthcare, in the Medinn blog and on its channels, and shared with clinicians, medical students and institutions.
  4. The insights add up. Across the 50 conversations, I’ll write about the gaps and opportunities in Kerala’s healthcare ecosystem, so that more people can understand it and contribute to it.
In the interest of transparency: Medinn is my consulting practice. If a conversation could lead to working together, I’ll say so plainly. It never changes how your story is told, and there is no fee or payment to be featured.
