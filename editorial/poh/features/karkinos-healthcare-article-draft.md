# Taking cancer care out of the four walls of the hospital
*People of Healthcare: Dr. Moni Abraham Kuriakose, Medical Director, Karkinos Healthcare*

Most cancer care in India still begins and ends inside a hospital. Karkinos Healthcare is built on a different idea: that cancer care can be distributed, and that the community itself can be a cancer centre.

**What Karkinos is building.** Karkinos has developed a technology platform that supports a patient through the whole journey: screening, diagnosis, treatment and follow-up, delivered in a distributed fashion, closer to where people live. Dr. Kuriakose says the platform has served around 3 million patients (his figure). Karkinos also runs a research and diagnostics laboratory in Kochi, the Advanced Center for Cancer Diagnostics and Research, which serves as a central lab for its network, with work focused on early diagnostic tools and molecular assays for precision medicine (source: Indian Pharma Post, June 2022).

**The bottleneck.** Two things held it back, he says. First, getting tertiary cancer centres, the large specialist hospitals, to adopt standardised care, so that a patient follows the same agreed pathway wherever they are treated. Second, once a patient has been treated, those centres have little incentive to send them back to a community cancer clinic for follow-up and continuing care closer to home. A distributed model only works if care actually flows back out into the community. *(Confirm with him that this is what he means.)*

**The bridges that need to be built.** Better adoption of digital health in primary health clinics, and adoption of the ABDM (Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission) system in hospitals. These are the connections that let a patient's record, and their care, move between a community clinic and a specialist centre.

**The vision.** At the heart of the model is a comprehensive cancer centre that anchors a network of community cancer clinics, so that quality care reaches people where they live, with a specialist centre behind every clinic.

**The future he sees.** Digital adoption in healthcare will keep increasing, and AI will help democratise quality cancer care, to the point where a patient can be as knowledgeable as a healthcare provider. What it takes: ABDM adoption and better health records. *(Confirm his exact wording.)*

### Fact note on "comprehensive cancer care is non-existent in Kerala"
Not supported. The Regional Cancer Centre, Thiruvananthapuram (founded 1981) and the Malabar Cancer Centre, Thalassery (set up 2001, mandate for comprehensive cancer care, recognised as a Tertiary Cancer Centre in 2012) are both described as comprehensive or tertiary cancer centres (sources below; mixed quality, check official documents before publishing). The fairer framing is the gap in access, follow-up and integration, which is what Dr. Kuriakose's own answers describe. Ask him rather than assert.
