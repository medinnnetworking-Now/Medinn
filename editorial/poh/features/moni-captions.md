## LinkedIn
Kerala has the highest cancer rate in the country. In 2024 it was about 170 new cases per lakh people, against a national average of around 110, which works out to roughly 61,000 new cases in a single year. Some of that is better screening, some of it is lifestyle. Nationally, the count has been climbing too.

I got to talk with Dr. Moni Abraham Kuriakose of Karkinos Healthcare about what that means for care. What stayed with me is where he starts: not at the big hospital, but at the primary level, the community clinic. Catch it early, treat it properly, and then make follow-up easy enough that the patient actually comes back to a clinic near home. His idea is a simple one: the community itself as a cancer centre.

We already screen for oral, breast and cervical cancer at primary health centres. What's much harder is everything after that: the referral, the treatment, and the follow-up back home. Dr. Moni told me the hard part has been getting large centres to adopt standardised care, and the lack of any real reason for them to send patients back to a community clinic. He sees ABDM and better use of AI as the way to close a lot of that gap. Karkinos has been building towards this, and he says they've served about 3 million patients so far.

People like Dr. Moni should be talked about more. Not to flatter anyone, but because the rest of us in healthcare can learn from them, support them and build on what they're doing. That's what People of Healthcare is for.
