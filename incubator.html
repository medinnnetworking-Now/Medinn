The idea

A hospital at the centre. Everyone who can build, around it.

Innovation in healthcare stalls when problems stay inside wards and builders stay outside them. This ecosystem puts one anchor hospital at the centre and connects it, permanently, to the people who can solve its problems.

  • The hospital brings real problems, clinical mentors, devices and a place to test.
  • Medical and engineering colleges, makers and startups bring ideas, skills and hands on the bench.
  • Medinn designs and runs the programme: partners, events, community and operations.

Scope

Four areas of work, one pipeline.

Each area stands on its own and is useful to the hospital from the first month. Together they take a problem from the ward all the way to a tested product.

01

Ideation

Finding the problems worth solving, with the people who face them every day.

What happens
A clinical problem bank fed by doctors, nurses, technicians and the biomedical team. Needs-finding rounds in departments, ideathons and problem-framing workshops.
Who is involved
Hospital clinicians and staff, medical students and residents, design and engineering students.
What the hospital gets
A documented, prioritized list of its own clinical and operational problems.

02

Prototyping

Turning the best problem statements into working first versions, quickly and cheaply.

What happens
Build sprints and medical hackathons on real problem statements, a maker bench with electronics and fabrication, and clinicians mentoring every team.
Who is involved
Engineering colleges, makerspaces, biomedical engineers and clinical mentors.
What the hospital gets
Working prototypes built against its own needs, not generic ones.

03

Repair cafés & biomedical training

Keeping devices in service, and teaching people how equipment actually works and fails.

What happens
Regular repair cafés on idle and condemned devices, device deconstruction labs, hands-on training in basic device care for nurses, technicians and students, and a low-cost maintenance tracker for every device.
Who is involved
The hospital biomedical team, engineers, makers, nursing and medical students.
What the hospital gets
Devices back in service, trained staff, and a clear record of device health.

04

New innovation

Taking what works from the bench to the ward, and from the ward to the market.

What happens
Clinical validation and pilots in the hospital with ethics in place, incubation of the strongest teams, and links to state startup support, investors and regulatory guidance.
Who is involved
Clinical leads, incubated teams, startup support bodies and funders.
What the hospital gets
First access to solutions it helped shape, and a name as the place where they began.

A first year

Start small, prove it, grow.

A proposed sequence, shaped with the anchor hospital. Each phase delivers something visible on its own.

Months 1–3 · Listen and repair

Problem bank and the first repair café

  • Needs-finding rounds across departments to build the problem bank.
  • A repair café with the biomedical team on idle or condemned devices.
  • Pilot the maintenance tracker on a set of devices.

Outcome: a prioritized problem bank and devices back in service.

Months 4–6 · Train and build

Training and build sprints

  • Biomedical training sessions on basic device care.
  • A medical hackathon on the top problem statements.
  • Clinicians mentor and judge; the strongest teams are shortlisted.

Outcome: trained staff and working prototypes.

Months 7–12 · Pilot and launch

Pilots and the first incubated teams

  • Validate one or two solutions in the hospital with ethics in place.
  • Incubate the strongest teams with startup support.
  • Share results as a regional innovation story.

Outcome: tested solutions and the first ventures.
