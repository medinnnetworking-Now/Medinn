The day

One bench. Two disciplines. Devices back to life.

Devices that had been condemned or left idle at Government Medical College, Kozhikode, were brought to TinkerSpace's bench. Clinicians explained how they are used and how they fail; engineers and makers opened them up, diagnosed and repaired.

On the bench

Two devices, two stories.

Case 01 · BPL patient monitor

Stuck on the boot screen. Back to showing vitals.

  1. It powered on, showed the brand logo, and went no further.
  2. The team suspected invisible micro-cracks in the solder joints, the kind pump vibration and cuff stress can cause over time.
  3. They applied flux, reflowed it with a hot-air rework station, and rechecked the capacitors with a multimeter.
  4. Switched back on, it booted past the logo to live vitals.

Case 02 · Mindray SV300 ventilator

Two faults found: the display and a motor in the gas-supply unit.

  1. Opened up and documented component by component.
  2. Display: fault.
  3. Gas-supply motor: fault.
  4. Along the way: a dust-laden intake filter and a hand-marked battery.

What we learned

The devices told us more than their faults.

No paper trail
Many devices had no record of maintenance contracts, servicing or routine care.
Donated, then lost
Donated devices often never get registered with the biomedical team, so they fall outside maintenance and warranty.
Care nobody taught
Basic care like filter changes is not something most healthcare workers are trained in.
Calibration
It is costly. How much of it actually happens?
